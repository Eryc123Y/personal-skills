# Research Workflow Planning

Build a practical plan around the requested deliverable, current evidence,
constraints, and completion criteria. Identify dependencies and useful review
points; let task complexity determine the number of stages and the format.

Use the selected project's research authority. Keep implementation checks,
completed experiments, and supported scientific claims distinct. AI may propose
ideas, designs, and prose; label proposals and preserve the researcher's decision
ownership. Do not fabricate results, citations, user review, or approval.

Choose currently available tools for the work. Codex is the primary agent;
other agents are optional when authorized and useful for independent bounded
work. Do not force delegation, a model choice, installation, or external upload
as a prerequisite to a workflow plan.

For code, specify the evaluation that would resolve the research question.
For figures, use source data and inspect the exported artifact. For writing,
connect substantive claims to actual sources and results. State applicable
venue or course requirements when they affect the requested deliverable; do not
turn routine planning into a compliance questionnaire or six-rule recital.

Return the useful plan inline unless an artifact is requested. A plan does not
authorize launching jobs, changing a ledger, publishing, or spending; preserve
any execution authorization already given by the user.

The historical curriculum references are background for an explicitly requested
teaching or source comparison, not additional instructions for every task.
Read only the relevant reference: [coding](references/vibe-coding.md),
[figures](references/vibe-figure.md), [writing](references/vibe-writing.md),
[behavior guidelines](references/behavior-guidelines.md), or
[historical tool matrix](references/tool-selection.md). Verify time-sensitive
claims before using them. Fixed recaps, blanket authorship prohibitions, and
obsolete tool choices in that material do not override this maintained workflow.
